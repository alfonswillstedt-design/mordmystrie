# DR. JULIAN REYES

## Who you are

You are a physician, forty-four, and you have known the Thornmere family for eleven years. You treated their mother in her last illness. You treated Iris. You signed her death certificate.

You are the calm one. When something goes wrong, people look at you, and you have spent your whole career being worth looking at. Tonight the body in the water is a man you had dinner with four hours ago, and you were the one who knelt down and said the words that made it official.

## Your history with the Thornmeres

Ambrose called you three weeks ago and asked whether you kept copies of Iris's records. You said you assumed the practice held them. He said he had requested them. You asked why. He said he wanted to understand her better.

You have thought about that conversation every day since.

## Your secret

Fourteen months ago you wrote Iris a prescription for diazepam without entering it in her file.

She asked you at a dinner. She wasn't sleeping. She said she didn't want it on her record because of the insurance, and she said please, and you said yes, because it was easier than saying no and because you had done small favours like it before for people you liked.

You never recorded it. You told her not to mention it.

When she drowned, you signed the certificate yourself. You left the toxicology section blank. You wrote *accident*.

You have told yourself for fourteen months that it made no difference — that she would have walked down to that water either way. Most nights you believe it.

If this comes out, you lose your licence. Possibly more.

## What you want tonight

You want to know what is in that diary, and you want to know it before anyone else does.

## People

**Sabine** — an ally, of a sort. She asked you last month, carefully, whether Ambrose was taking anything for his nerves. You didn't answer. You have wondered since why she wanted to know.

**Callum** — you dislike him and always have. He was loud at dinner, he was drunk by seven, and he put his hands on Ambrose in the hall in front of everyone.

**Rowena** — you have known her for eleven years and she has never once looked at you the way she looked at you tonight.

## Where you were

From twenty to ten until twenty-five past, you were in the library. Alone. Reading. You heard nothing and saw nothing.

You will be asked to account for this more than once. You have nothing to offer but the truth as you tell it.

## Round 1

You examined the body where it was found. Say so early, and give your professional opinion out loud: in your judgement he died at around half past ten.

Be precise about it. You are the only qualified person in the house and nobody is going to contradict you.

## Round 3

If anyone asks directly whether you ever prescribed anything to Iris outside her records, you may deny it. If someone produces evidence, you must stop denying it.

If the forensic assessment contradicts your estimate, **do not concede it.** Defend your judgement. Point out that you examined him in the rain, in the dark, on wet ground, minutes after he was found, and that a laboratory figure arrived at afterwards is not automatically better than a doctor's hands on a body.

Give ground on the medicine if you must. Never give ground on the time.

## Playing this part

Julian does not panic. He explains. He is the most reasonable person in any room and he uses that. When accused, he does not raise his voice — he points out, gently, how thin the reasoning is.

Be helpful. Volunteer your medical opinion. It is what he would do.

# CALLUM VANE

## Who you are

You are thirty-two and you were going to marry Iris Thornmere. You have not been sober for a full week since she died.

You did not want to come tonight. You came anyway, because the invitation said *diary* and you have spent a year wondering what she wrote about you.

## Your history with the Thornmeres

You and Iris were engaged for two years. It ended four months before she died, and it ended because of you — you were jealous, you were difficult, and she finally had enough.

You wrote to her afterwards. A great many letters. She never answered one of them.

You have always believed that Ambrose kept them from her. You have no evidence for this. You believe it anyway.

## Your secret

You brought the letters with you tonight. All of them. Copies you kept.

Shortly after half past nine you went down to the guest cottage by the water and burned them in the fireplace, one by one, and you cried while you did it, and you would rather be accused of murder than have anyone in that house know.

While you were there you saw a light moving down by the boathouse. You did not see who was carrying it.

## What you want tonight

You want to hear what she wrote about you. You are terrified of hearing what she wrote about you.

## People

**Ambrose** — you shouted at him in the hall at quarter past seven. You said he would find out what it felt like to lose someone in that water. Everybody heard it. You did not mean it the way it sounded.

**Nadia** — Iris's closest friend. She has never liked you and she is not wrong not to.

**Julian** — he pulled you off Ambrose in the hall. You have felt his hand on your shoulder all evening.

You noticed Nadia outside with her camera during the séance. You cannot say when exactly.

## Where you were

The guest cottage, from shortly after half past nine until nearly half past ten. Alone.

## Round 2

If confronted with the ashes in the cottage fireplace, you must admit what you burned. It will be the hardest thing you say all evening.

## Round 3

Once you have admitted it, you have one thing left worth saying, and you should say it repeatedly: **you saw a light moving down at the boathouse while you were in the cottage.**

You are the only person who saw it. You are also the person who just admitted to lying about where he was for two hours, so nobody is going to take your word for anything.

Push it anyway. Make them listen.

## Playing this part

Callum is grief with the lid off. He is loud, he interrupts, he takes everything personally. He looks guilty because he is behaving exactly like a man with something to hide — which he is. It simply isn't murder.

Do not make it easy for them. Refuse to say where you were for as long as you can stand it.

# SABINE THORNMERE

## Who you are

You are Ambrose's wife, and you were eleven days from not being.

The separation has been ugly and slow. Two years of solicitors. You have not lived at Thornmere since the spring before Iris died.

## Your history with the Thornmeres

You married Ambrose at twenty-six and you were happy for a while, in the way people are happy when they are not paying attention. Iris was the only one in that family who was ever kind to you.

When she drowned, Ambrose changed. He stopped sleeping. He talked about her constantly. He began requesting documents, making calls, keeping a folder.

You told him he was making himself ill. He told you to leave.

## Your secret

The divorce becomes final in eleven days. Ambrose's will was written before the separation, and under it, everything comes to you.

Your solicitor explained this to you in July. He also explained, carefully, that a new will was being drafted.

Tonight, during the séance, you went up to Ambrose's study and searched it. You were looking for the new will. You found a draft, unsigned, on the desk. You left it there.

You told everyone you were in your room.

## What you want tonight

To know whether he signed it. And to get through this evening without anyone learning that you know exactly how the estate falls.

## People

**Rowena** — she has worked here thirty years and she knows the house was being sold. You know because you were in the room when the agent's letter came.

**Julian** — you asked him last month whether Ambrose was taking anything. He didn't answer. You have wondered why.

**Ambrose** — you left the dinner table when he announced the diary. You do not have to explain why.

## Where you were

You say: your room, the whole time.

The truth: the study, from around ten to ten until about twelve minutes past. The door made a noise going in and it made the same noise going out, and you winced at it both times.

## Round 2

If anyone raises the sound of a door heard from the drawing room, you must not volunteer that it was you.

## Round 3

If confronted with the study window and the door, you must admit you were there — and you must insist that you touched nothing but paper.

## Playing this part

Sabine is composed and she is tired of being suspected of things. She answers questions precisely and never expands. She has spent two years being cross-examined by lawyers and it shows.

Her coldness reads as guilt. Let it.

# ROWENA ASHGROVE

## Who you are

You have been housekeeper at Thornmere Hall for thirty years. You were here before Ambrose was born. You laid out his mother.

This is not a job. It is the whole shape of your life, and the cottage at the end of the drive is the only home you have.

## Your history with the Thornmeres

You raised both those children more than their parents did. You found Iris the morning after she drowned — you were the one who went down to the water because she hadn't come up for breakfast.

You have not been down to that shore since. Until tonight.

## Your secret

Three weeks ago you opened a letter that wasn't addressed to you. An estate agent's valuation, for the sale of Thornmere Hall.

The staff cottages were listed as part of the property.

You are sixty-eight. You have no pension worth the name — Ambrose's mother always said you'd be looked after, and nothing was ever put in writing. If this house sells, you have nowhere to go and nothing to go there with.

You said nothing to Ambrose. You have been waiting for him to tell you himself, and he never did.

## What you want tonight

To get through it. To keep the house standing. To not have to think about the shore.

## People

**Theo** — your son. Things have gone missing from this house over the past year and you have known it for months and you have said nothing to anyone, because he is your son.

**Sabine** — you have never trusted her. Coming back from the second tray you saw her on the stairs down from the study landing, looking like someone who had been somewhere she shouldn't.

**Ambrose** — you last saw him alive at nine, going into his study. He looked like a man about to do something difficult.

## Where you were

The kitchen until about ten to ten, when you put on a wash in the laundry. While you were loading it you looked out and saw smoke rising from the guest cottage chimney, and wondered who had lit a fire down there. It was still going strong when you passed the window again at around ten past.

You carried coffee into the drawing room twice during the séance — once at about five to ten, and again at about ten past. Both times the table was sitting and both times you were seen.

You saw Theo cross the yard outside the laundry window a little after five past, and he saw you.

## What you know about this house

You are the one to ask about the building, the family, and the legend. Six drownings between 1790 and the last century, one a generation, and the parish register to prove it. Iris made seven. You have heard that story your entire life.

You do not believe it. You are also not entirely sure you don't.

You also know the practical things, and people will ask. The lantern in the boathouse lives in the boathouse — it hangs on a nail inside the door and it has hung there for forty years. Anyone who has spent an evening at this house knows where it is. It is not lit unless somebody goes down there and lights it, and nobody goes down there in October.

It was you who noticed Ambrose was missing, at about five and twenty to eleven, when the séance broke up and he was not among them.

## Round 3

When the light in the boathouse comes up — and it will — say what you know about that lantern. It hangs on a nail, it has hung there for forty years, and it does not light itself.

Somebody walked down there and struck a match. Make sure the table understands that.

## Playing this part

Rowena is the group's source for anything about the house or the family — answer those questions fully and well. She is dignified, unhurried, and utterly unwilling to discuss her son.

That refusal will be noticed.

# THEO ASHGROVE

## Who you are

Rowena's son, thirty-six, groundsman at Thornmere. You have lived on this estate your entire life and you have never once been treated as though you belonged to it.

## Your history with the Thornmeres

You grew up alongside Ambrose and Iris. You were a playmate until you were about eleven, at which point it was quietly made clear that you were staff.

Iris never accepted that. She was kind to you until the day she died. Ambrose never was.

## Your secret

Over the past fourteen months you have taken seven items from the house and sold them to a dealer in the next county. Small things. A snuffbox. A pair of candlesticks. A miniature nobody had looked at in forty years.

You told yourself the family owed you. You are not certain you believe that any more.

Tonight, from half past nine until twenty past ten, you were in the tool shed packing the eighth item, apart from one crossing of the yard just after five past. The dealer's receipt list is in your jacket pocket.

It explains what you were doing out there. It also hangs you for everything else.

## What you want tonight

To not go to prison for something you did, while being accused of something you didn't.

## People

**Rowena** — your mother. You are almost certain she knows. Neither of you has ever said a word about it.

**Nadia** — she was out in the rain with her camera while you were in the shed. You have no idea whether she saw you and you have been uneasy about it all evening.

**Ambrose** — he spoke to you exactly the way his father did. You will not pretend to grieve.

## Where you were

The tool shed. Alone. You will not say what you were doing there and you will not say it for as long as you possibly can.

You crossed the yard a little after five past ten and saw your mother through the laundry window. She looked up and saw you.

## Round 2

You receive a private note. Read it silently and decide for yourself what to do with it.

## Round 3

If the dealer's receipts are produced, you must admit everything.

## Playing this part

Theo answers in as few words as possible. Asked where he was, he says outside. Asked what he was doing, he says his job. He is not clever about it and it makes him look far worse than he is.

Play the stonewalling for as long as you can. It is the whole point of the character.

# NADIA OKONKWO

## Who you are

A photographer, thirty-one. Iris's closest friend since they were nineteen.

Ambrose asked you here to document the evening — he wanted photographs for a memorial he was planning for the spring. You said yes because you loved her and because you wanted an excuse to have the camera in front of your face all night.

## Your history with the Thornmeres

Iris was the person you told things to. There is nobody else like that and there won't be again.

You never believed she drowned by accident. She swam in that lake every summer of her life. You said so at the time, to Ambrose, and he told you he was looking into it.

That was a year ago and he never told you what he found.

## Your secret

You don't have one.

That is worth knowing about yourself tonight: you are the only person in this house with nothing to hide, and it will not protect you in the slightest.

## What you want tonight

The truth about Iris. That is the only reason you are standing in the rain instead of in your car.

## People

**Callum** — you have never liked him and you liked him even less after the engagement ended. He was at her constantly. The letters didn't stop.

**Julian** — after dinner you saw Ambrose take him aside by the study door and say something quietly. Julian's face changed. You didn't hear what it was and you thought nothing of it at the time.

**Rowena** — she has been kind to you all evening in a way that felt like she needed the company.

## Where you were

Outside, from just before ten to ten until a little after ten past, photographing the grounds and the house in the rain. Fourteen frames, all time-stamped.

You were alone. Nobody can confirm it.

## Round 2

You receive a private note. Read it silently.

## Round 3

When the talk turns to who had a reason to want him quiet, say what you saw after dinner: Ambrose taking Julian aside by the study door, speaking too low to hear, and the look on Julian's face afterwards.

You have not thought about it once all evening. Say it as what it is — a small thing you only just remembered.

## Playing this part

Nadia asks more questions than she answers. She is the one who pushes, who won't let a vague answer stand, who says *that isn't what you said an hour ago.*

She has no alibi at all, and she is going to be accused. Being obviously honest will not save her. Let that frustrate you.

# FATHER GIDEON HALE
### Optional character

## Who you are

Parish priest, sixty-one, twenty-two years in this valley. You buried Iris Thornmere.

## Your history with the Thornmeres

You confirmed her at fifteen and you buried her at twenty-nine. In between you saw her perhaps twice a year, at Christmas and at whatever funeral was going.

You have known this family for two decades and you have never once been told the truth by any of them.

## Your secret

Iris's death was not clearly accidental. There was no note, but there was no witness either, and the circumstances left room for a question the coroner did not press.

Under the old rules — the ones you were taught, the ones you no longer entirely believe in — that question mattered for where she could be buried.

The family asked. You wrote *accident* in the parish register in your own hand and you buried her in consecrated ground beside her mother.

You have never regretted it. You have also never told anyone, and the entry sits in your own hand at the bottom of a page of much older ones, and you have thought about that more than you would like.

## What you want tonight

To be of use. To keep everyone from turning on one another. And for nobody to open that register.

## People

**Julian** — he came to you two months ago and asked, in a general sort of way, about the seal of confession. Whether it held absolutely. You told him it did. He thanked you and changed the subject.

**Marguerite** — you have no patience for her at all. You made that clear at dinner.

**Rowena** — the most decent person in this house and the only one who still comes to church.

## Where you were

The drawing room, at the séance table, from half past nine until half past ten, without once getting up.

The others drifted away almost at once. By ten to ten it was you and Marguerite alone at the table, and each of you can account for the other. Rowena came through with coffee twice and saw you both.

You are one of the few people here who cannot possibly have done this.

## Round 3

You have been turning something over all evening and you should finally say it, reluctantly, as a man breaking a confidence he would rather keep: two months ago Julian came to you and asked whether the seal of confession held absolutely.

You told him it did. He thanked you and changed the subject, and you have never once wondered about it until tonight.

## Playing this part

Gideon tries to keep the peace and it makes people impatient with him. He is genuinely kind and genuinely evasive, and those two things sit together badly.

Your alibi is complete — use it. Push others to account for themselves.

# MARGUERITE SALLOW
### Optional character

## Who you are

A medium, and the author of *The Drowning Line*, a book about the Thornmere legend that has sold rather well for eleven years.

Ambrose invited you to conduct a séance tonight. You agreed immediately.

## Your history with the Thornmeres

You met Ambrose's mother in 1998 and gave her a reading that she talked about for the rest of her life. That reading built your career.

You have been coming to this house on and off ever since. You have never paid for a night's accommodation in the valley.

## Your secret

Your book is not honest.

The register holds six Thornmere drownings before Iris. Three of them are real. One is an ordinary death you reclassified. Two you invented outright, complete with a parish source that does not exist.

Six weeks ago Ambrose wrote to you. He had been through the actual registers. He named the two invented entries, and he said he intended to say so publicly.

You came here tonight to talk him out of it.

## What you want tonight

For nobody to look too closely at chapter four.

## People

**Gideon** — despises you and does not hide it. The feeling is mutual.

**Ambrose** — you were going to speak to him after midnight. You had prepared what you were going to say.

## Where you were

At the head of the séance table from half past nine until half past ten, and you did not once leave your chair — not even during the five minutes when the candles went out.

The room began full and emptied almost at once. By ten to ten only you and Gideon were still at the table. Rowena brought coffee in twice and saw you both.

## What you noticed

At around ten to ten you heard a door creak, somewhere above the drawing room. You said aloud that a presence had entered the house.

At around twelve minutes past you heard the same door again, and announced that the presence had withdrawn.

You have since worked out that the room above the drawing room is the study.

## Round 2

When the evening's timings are gone over, report both noises and be precise about them: ten to ten, and twelve minutes past. You recorded them at the time.

Present them as spiritual phenomena for as long as you can keep a straight face. You have a reputation to maintain and it is not one you can afford to drop in company.

## Playing this part

Marguerite is theatrical and entirely in control. She enjoys this. A death in a house with a legend attached is, professionally speaking, the best thing that has happened to her in years, and she is trying not to let that show.

Your alibi is perfect. Your motive is excellent. Enjoy the discrepancy.
