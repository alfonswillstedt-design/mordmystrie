# ENVELOPE TEXTS

---

## ENVELOPE ONE

*Read aloud once everyone has finished their booklet.*

It is twenty to eleven on the night of the twenty-sixth of October.

You are standing in the rain below Thornmere Hall, looking at a dead man.

Ambrose Thornmere is lying at the waterline, face down in the mud, his feet in the lake. The back of his head is crushed. A cast-iron mooring bollard lies a metre away, half sunk. The watch on his wrist is smashed and has stopped, but the hands have been torn away and nobody can read a time from it.

Twenty metres from here, exactly one year ago, they pulled his sister Iris out of the same water.

You all know each other. You were all invited here by the man lying in front of you, and you all received the same letter: he had found Iris's diary, and he intended to read from it at midnight.

The road embankment gave way at a quarter past eight. The telephone line died at twenty to ten. There is no signal in this valley and there never has been.

Nobody has come here since this afternoon. Nobody has left.

Which means one of you killed him.

You are not getting out until the water drops, and that will take hours. You can stand here and wait, or you can find out what happened.

**Now:** deal the seven items marked 1 and read each aloud. Then you have twenty-five minutes to question one another.

You may lie about anything except what is printed on an evidence card.

End the round with each person naming who they suspect and why.

---

## ENVELOPE TWO

*Read aloud after round one.*

You have carried him into the library and laid him on the floor under a sheet.

Rowena has made coffee because somebody had to do something. The rain has not eased. Through the windows the boathouse is a dark shape down by the water, and nobody wants to say out loud that the lantern down there is still burning.

Someone starts counting backwards. What were you doing at ten? Where were you standing? Who saw you?

It turns out that nine o'clock is a time everyone can account for. Ten o'clock is another matter entirely.

The séance ran in the drawing room from half past nine until half past ten. Two people sat at that table without once leaving it. The rest came and went, and nobody thought to keep score.

And somewhere in that half hour, Ambrose walked down to the water.

He did not go by chance. He went because somebody asked him to.

**Now:** deal the eight items marked 2 and read them aloud. Give the two small envelopes to the named players — they read in silence and decide for themselves what to say.

Thirty minutes. This round is about time. Where exactly were you, and who can confirm it?

End the round with each person naming who they suspect and why.

---

## ENVELOPE THREE

*Read aloud after round two.*

It is past midnight now.

Nobody has said it out loud, but you have all thought it: he would have read from the diary at twelve.

Rowena found it in the study, undamaged, sitting on the desk. It was out. He had taken it out to bring it down with him.

What is on the table in front of you is no longer guesswork. It is a body that cooled at a measurable rate, a window that does not open, a fire that somebody lit, and a set of papers that say where people actually were.

Four of you are about to stop having to defend yourselves.

Two of you are not.

**Now:** deal the seven items marked 3 and read them aloud.

Thirty minutes. Go back over what was said in rounds one and two. Somebody gave an account that no longer holds.

When the time is up, everyone completes a verdict card.

# EVIDENCE CARDS

Cut apart. Keep sorted by round. Do not read while preparing.

---

## ROUND ONE

**1 — THE SCENE**

Ambrose Thornmere lies at the waterline, face down, feet in the lake. The back of the skull is crushed. A cast-iron mooring bollard rests a metre away, partly sunk in the mud. His pockets are empty.

The watch on his wrist is broken against the stone edging. The crystal is shattered and both hands have been torn from the movement. No time can be read from it.

---

**2 — THE INVITATION**

Handwritten by Ambrose. Identical to all eight.

*I have found Iris's diary. I intend to read from it at midnight. I believe every one of you should be present when I do.*

---

**3 — THE DEATH CERTIFICATE**

Iris Thornmere, aged 29.
Cause of death: drowning.
Circumstances: accident.
Signed: Dr. Julian Reyes, 27th October, last year.

The toxicology section has been left blank.

---

**4 — THE PARISH REGISTER**

Six drownings recorded in the Thornmere family between 1790 and the last century — roughly one a generation.

Iris is the seventh, entered at the foot of the page in a hand much more recent than those above it. A note in the margin reads: *consecrated ground, at the family's request.*

---

**5 — THE WILL**

Ambrose's will predates the separation. On his death, Sabine inherits the entire estate.

A draft of a new will lies unsigned on the desk beside it.

---

**6 — ACCOUNT OF THE ARGUMENT**

Rowena's written recollection of the hall, 7:15 p.m.

Callum said something to the effect that Ambrose would find out what it felt like to lose someone in that lake. Several people heard it. Julian and Theo separated them.

---

**7 — THE AGENT'S LETTER**

Opened. Addressed to Ambrose. A valuation of Thornmere Hall ahead of sale.

One paragraph notes that the staff cottages are included in the lot.

---

## ROUND TWO

**8 — THE SÉANCE RECORD**

Marguerite's own note.

Began 9:30 p.m. Interrupted 10:00 p.m. when the candles went out. Resumed 10:05 p.m. Ended 10:30 p.m.

Present throughout without leaving the table: Marguerite Sallow, Father Gideon Hale. Others came and went.

Mrs Ashgrove brought coffee into the room at approximately 9:55 p.m. and again at approximately 10:10 p.m. She was seen on both occasions.

---

**9 — THE TELEPHONE LOG**

Last outgoing call 9:38 p.m., duration twelve seconds. The number belongs to a firm of solicitors.

The line fails at 9:40 p.m.

---

**10 — THE NOTE**

Recovered from a gap in the boathouse floorboards. Water-damaged. Block capitals.

*BOATHOUSE. TEN TO TEN. COME ALONE — THIS CONCERNS HER DEATH.*

---

**11 — NADIA'S CONTACT SHEET**

Fourteen frames, time-stamped, between 9:48 p.m. and 10:12 p.m.

Frame four, taken at 9:56 p.m., shows the kitchen garden. At the edge of the frame the tool shed door stands open with a lamp lit inside, and a figure is visible at the bench.

Frame nine, taken at 10:04 p.m., shows the south face of the house in the rain. In the lower left corner the boathouse is visible.

**There is a light burning in the boathouse window.**

Frame twelve, taken at 10:09 p.m., is another view of the kitchen garden. The tool shed door is still open, the lamp is still lit, and the same figure is at the bench.

---

**12 — JULIAN'S ACCOUNT**

Taken down on the night.

He states he was in the library from approximately 9:40 p.m. until 10:25 p.m., alone, reading. He heard nothing and saw nothing.

He further states, having examined the body at 10:50 p.m., that in his opinion death occurred at around 10:30 p.m.

---

**13 — GROUND FLOOR PLAN**

The library occupies the south-east corner. Two windows. One faces directly down towards the boathouse — forty metres, unobstructed.

---

**14 — THE SOUND IN THE STUDY**

Marguerite's marginal note on the séance record.

A door was heard to creak somewhere above the drawing room, at approximately ten to ten. She recorded it as manifestation.

A second creak was heard from the same direction at approximately twelve minutes past ten. She recorded that the presence had withdrawn.

---

**15 — THE COTTAGE FIREPLACE**

Ash and partly burned paper in the guest cottage grate. Handwritten. One legible fragment:

*...never should have let you go back to that house...*

---

### PRIVATE NOTE — NADIA

*Read silently.*

You remember changing lenses at around ten past ten, and that as you did you thought you saw a light moving down by the water.

You thought nothing of it at the time.

---

### PRIVATE NOTE — THEO

*Read silently.*

The dealer's receipt list is in your jacket pocket. It explains what you were doing out there.

It also hangs you for everything else.

---

## ROUND THREE

**16 — THE WASHING MACHINE**

Cycle counter shows a programme started at 9:52 p.m. and completed at 10:41 p.m.

Mrs Ashgrove states that while loading the machine she looked out of the laundry window and saw smoke coming from the guest cottage chimney. She places this at a little before ten and remarks that she wondered who had lit a fire down there.

She states that it was still smoking when she passed the window again at around ten past, and that the smoke was thick both times, as it is when paper is being fed onto a fire rather than left to burn down.

---

**17 — FORENSIC ASSESSMENT**

Based on body temperature, the progression of rigor, and an ambient temperature of ten degrees, death occurred between 9:50 p.m. and 10:15 p.m.

The margin of error is stated as narrow.

---

**18 — THE BOLLARD**

Cast iron. 4.2 kilograms. Blood and hair on one edge.

No usable prints — it has been in water. The blow came from above and slightly from the left.

---

**19 — THE DEALER'S RECEIPTS**

Produced by Theo Ashgrove from his jacket pocket, under questioning.

A handwritten list of eight items with dates, running back fourteen months. Seven have been collected and settled.

The eighth is dated today and marked as awaiting collection.

---

**20 — THE STUDY WINDOW**

Screwed shut from the inside, and has been for years. The only way in is the door.

The door creaks audibly into the drawing room below.

---

**21 — THE MEDICAL FILE**

A copy from Iris Thornmere's patient record. Two pages are missing — the numbering runs from 14 to 17.

In the margin of the preceding page, in Ambrose's hand:

*diazepam — not in the record. Ask J.*

---

**22 — THE DIARY**

Found in the study, undamaged. The final entry is dated three days before her death.

*He said it would help me sleep. He said not to tell anyone he had given it to me.*

# INVITATION

*Send to guests in advance. Replace the bracketed details.*

---

**THORNMERE HALL**

*[Guest name]*

It has been a year.

On the twenty-sixth of October I am opening the house for one evening. I am asking the same people who were here that autumn to come back, and I am asking without much grace, because I do not think any of you will want to.

I have found Iris's diary.

I have read it. I intend to read from it aloud at midnight, and I believe every one of you should be present when I do.

Come at six. Stay the night — the valley road is unreliable in October and I would rather not have anyone driving it late.

I am not asking you to dress for dinner. I am asking you to come.

*Ambrose Thornmere*
